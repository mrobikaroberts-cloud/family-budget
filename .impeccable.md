## Design Context

### Users
Married couple managing household finances together. Both spouses use the app equally — one sets up the monthly plan, both check in weekly. Used on laptop and iPad primarily, must work on mobile. The non-setup spouse needs to read the financial picture instantly without context.

### Brand Personality
Calm · Trustworthy · Together — a tool that makes money feel manageable rather than anxious. Professional enough to take seriously, warm enough to feel shared. Monarch Money is the closest public reference.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Clean light theme: off-white canvas, white cards, very subtle shadows — never dark-by-default
- Monarch Money inspired but not derivative: same clarity and restraint, different typographic personality
- Typography: Manrope at weight 300 for large numbers, 400 for body, 500 for labels — light and airy
- Primary green `oklch(49% 0.165 145)` for positive/available states
- Amber `oklch(68% 0.16 75)` for warnings, coral-red `oklch(53% 0.22 27)` for over-budget
- Fixed expenses (rent, utilities, subscriptions) styled in neutral slate — feel "heavy" to motivate reduction
- All OKLCH colors, neutrals tinted toward brand green hue

### Strategic Goals (informs design priorities)
1. Short-term: Reduce fixed expenses (make them visible and "heavy" in the UI)
2. Medium-term: Consistent savings toward goals
3. Long-term: Increase income → 50/30/20 split as aspirational compass

### Design Principles
1. **True Available first** — the hero metric is always "how much can we actually spend?" (income minus spent, minus upcoming fixed expenses not yet paid). Not net cash flow. Not balance.
2. **Fixed vs variable is sacred** — fixed commitments (rent, utilities, subscriptions) are always visually distinct from variable spending (groceries, dining, gas). Different behavioral goals.
3. **Remaining, not spent** — frame progress bars as "$X remaining" not "$X spent." Positive framing reduces anxiety for shared viewers.
4. **Spouse-readable at a glance** — every screen must communicate its key insight to someone who didn't set up the budget, without tooltips or explanation.
5. **50/30/20 as compass, not judge** — the aspirational split appears in Insights as a trend line, never as a live failure state on the home screen.

### Navigation (4 tabs)
Home | Plan | Bills | Insights

### Anti-patterns to avoid
- No border-left accent stripes (use icons, background tints, or typography weight instead)
- No gradient text
- No nested cards inside cards
- No rings/donut charts outside of the Bills tab
- No sparklines used decoratively
- Progress bars: max 8px height, always show remaining amount alongside percentage
- No glassmorphism (previous session used this — it was wrong for this use case)
- Fonts NOT to use: Bricolage Grotesque, Inter, Plus Jakarta Sans, DM Sans, Outfit, Instrument Sans (all rejected)
- Font in use: Manrope (light weights 300/400/500)
